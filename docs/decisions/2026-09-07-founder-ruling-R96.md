## What was asked, and what makes this ruling different from R89

The founder was walked through the `stop` option and asked *why we would stop the loop*. Answering that
question forced a correction the supervisor owed him: **`stop` had been mis-framed on his decision page as
"the identity loop closes."** That is only its effect. `stop` is a **pre-registered kill criterion** from
[`ROADMAP.md` § Kill criteria](../../ROADMAP.md), whose signal is *"a capability predicate gets merged as a
duplicate"* and whose reading is *"The registry is flattening true distinctions. **Stop** — this is the
failure that destroys meaning."* Saying `stop` rules that **the merge-centred design is wrong and should be
redesigned rather than shipped.** The correction was made on the page before the founder answered.

In the same message the supervisor named the gap in its own recommendation, and it is the reason a fourth
option existed to choose:

> Twenty-three trips have each been ruled *"implementation defect, not design; row stays armed"* — by the
> supervisor who found that trip, one at a time, in the moment. **Nobody has ever read the series as a
> series.** Twenty-three bugs and one design defect found twenty-three times look identical from inside any
> single trip.

The founder chose `audit`.

## What is ruled

1. **`stop` is NOT taken — and it is NOT declined.** This is the material difference from R89 and from the
   fourteen supervisor recommendations before it. The fifteenth put **stands open**, unanswered, pending
   the audit's finding. The register must not record a sixteenth decline; there has not been one.
   *(Supervisor's reading of a one-word ruling, stated so the founder can correct it: choosing the option
   whose stated purpose was "to let you answer `stop` on evidence rather than on instinct" is a deferral of
   the answer, not the answer. If the founder meant `stop` declined outright, say so and this clause is
   struck.)*

2. **The register audit opens as row 6e.** Numbered in the 6-series because R89 made the kill row's home a
   dedicated row and this is 6d's direct successor; the founder may renumber it.

3. **Row 6e writes NO product code.** Not a fix, not an amendment, not a test of the surface. Its output is
   a finding document and a ruling. Any defect it notices is *recorded and routed*, never fixed in this row
   — fixing is what every previous row did, and it is precisely the behaviour under audit.

4. **The audit's question, in one sentence, fixed here so the row cannot drift off it:** *are the
   twenty-three kill-row trips twenty-three implementation defects, or one design defect found twenty-three
   times?*

5. **The audit must be able to return an answer that costs the project.** A row that can only conclude
   "twenty-three bugs" is not an audit. Its brief carries a pre-registered falsifier and both verdicts are
   real outcomes; if it finds one design defect, the honest consequence is that `stop` fires and the
   merge-centred shape goes back on the table.
